Frequently Asked Questions

Speech Rate, also known as Zero Crossing Rate (ZCR), measures how quickly a person is speaking during a call. A balanced speech rate indicates clarity and calmness, while a very high or low rate may signal stress, confusion, or disengagement. Monitoring this helps assess agent composure and communication effectiveness.

Valence measures the positivity or negativity of emotion (happy vs. sad). Arousal reflects the energy or excitement level (calm vs. excited). Dominance indicates the sense of control or assertiveness. Together, VAD scores provide a detailed emotional profile of the conversation, helping to understand both agent and customer states.

Sentiment Analysis uses natural language processing to evaluate the emotional tone of each message in the transcript. It classifies segments as positive, neutral, or negative, and calculates overall sentiment percentages for both agent and customer. This helps track satisfaction and identify areas for improvement.

The Calm Score is a measure of how calm or composed the agent sounds during the call, based on audio features. A higher calm score suggests a more relaxed and professional tone, which is desirable for customer interactions.

The Overall Score combines multiple factors: context-based sentiment, tone (calmness), SOP adherence, agent sentiment, and resolution status. Each component is weighted to reflect its importance, providing a holistic measure of call quality and agent performance.

SOP Adherence checks if the agent followed Standard Operating Procedures, such as identity verification, security questions, and best practices. High adherence means the agent is compliant with company protocols, reducing risk and improving service quality.

The Sentiment Flow chart visualizes how the emotional tone changes throughout the call, message by message. It helps identify key moments of positivity or negativity, agent impact, and customer reactions in real time.
